Budget 101

How to spend your money where it actually matters.

A good PC build isn’t about spending more, it’s about spending smart. This page shows you where budget has the biggest impact, where you can safely save money, and where cutting corners usually causes regret.

The Golden Rule of Budgeting

Spend for your use case
“Purpose first”

Your budget should reflect what you actually do on your PC. A gaming build, a school/work PC, and a creative workstation all prioritize different parts. Overspending on the wrong component doesn’t make the PC better, it just makes it unbalanced.

Simple takeaway: Decide what you use the PC for before you decide how much to spend.
Gaming focus
GPU > CPU
Work / school
CPU + RAM
Creative work
CPU + GPU balance

CPU

Don’t overspend
“Good enough”

Many beginners overspend on the CPU. For most gaming and everyday tasks, mid-range CPUs perform extremely well. Expensive CPUs only matter if your workload actually uses all those extra cores.

Simple takeaway: A mid-range CPU is rarely the bottleneck.
Safe to save
gaming builds
Spend more if
rendering / compiling
Beginner tip
avoid flagship CPUs

GPU

Biggest impact for gaming
“FPS driver”

For gaming, the GPU has the largest effect on performance. If your main goal is smooth gameplay, this is usually where the largest portion of the budget should go.

Simple takeaway: If you’re gaming, prioritize GPU before almost anything else.
Biggest impact
gaming performance
Common mistake
overpaying for CPU instead
Beginner tip
match GPU to monitor

RAM

Capacity > speed
“Enough first”

RAM speed has diminishing returns. Having enough RAM matters far more than having the fastest RAM. For most users, 16GB is the sweet spot.

Simple takeaway: More RAM beats faster RAM for most people.
Best value
16GB kits
Safe to skip
extreme speeds
Beginner tip
buy matched kits

Storage

Fast enough is enough
“SSD first”

Any modern SSD will feel fast for everyday use. Ultra-high-end drives offer diminishing real world benefits for most users.

Simple takeaway: Prioritize SSD capacity over premium speeds.
Must-have
SSD for OS
Safe to save
top-tier NVMe
Beginner tip
1TB is comfy

PSU

Do not cheap out
“Safety”

A bad PSU can cause crashes or damage parts. While you don’t need an expensive unit, you should never buy the cheapest option.

Simple takeaway: A good PSU protects every other component.
Never cheap out
PSU quality
Look for
80+ Bronze or better
Beginner tip
buy reputable brands

Case

Airflow over looks
“Comfort”

You don’t need an expensive case, but you do want decent airflow and enough space to build comfortably. Fancy glass doesn’t improve performance.

Simple takeaway: A well-ventilated case beats a flashy one.
Safe to save
case aesthetics
Don’t skip
airflow
Beginner tip
mesh fronts help

Monitor

The most visible upgrade
“Experience”

A great monitor can make a mid-range PC feel amazing. A weak monitor can make a powerful PC feel underwhelming.

Simple takeaway: Don’t ignore the monitor, it’s part of the budget.
Big impact
resolution + refresh rate
Common mistake
overspending inside PC
