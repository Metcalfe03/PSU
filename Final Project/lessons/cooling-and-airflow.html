Cooling & Airflow

Keep temps reasonable, keep noise low, don’t overthink it.

Move cool air in, move warm air out, and don’t block the path.

The Goal of Airflow

What you’re trying to accomplish
“Simple”

Your PC parts produce heat. Fans don’t “make cold air,” They move air. Airflow is just moving warm air away from components so they don’t bake inside the case.

Good airflow usually means: front/bottom intake (air comes in), rear/top exhaust (air goes out), and a clear path through the middle.

Simple takeaway: Air in the front, air out the back/top, keep the middle clear.
Intake
front / bottom fans
Exhaust
rear / top fans
Bonus
clean cable routing

Case Matters More Than People Think

Mesh vs “sealed glass box”
“Air”

The easiest way to have good cooling is picking a case that can breathe. A front mesh panel usually performs better than a solid front panel.

Simple takeaway: If the case can’t pull in air, no fan setup will “fix” it.
Best for airflow
mesh front panel
Watch out for
solid front panels
Beginner tip
dust filters help

A “Good Enough” Fan Setup

What to do if you want an easy answer
“Baseline”

Most beginner builds are totally fine with: 2 intake fans in the front and 1 exhaust fan in the rear. If your case already comes with fans, you might not need to buy more immediately.

Simple takeaway: 2 front intake + 1 rear exhaust is a great default.
Minimum
1 intake + 1 exhaust
Sweet spot
2 intake + 1 exhaust
Extra fans
nice, not mandatory

Positive vs Negative Pressure

The dust explanation
“Dust”

This sounds technical, but it’s simple: if you have a bit more intake than exhaust, air tends to leave through cracks instead of sucking dust in through them. That usually means a cleaner case over time.

Simple takeaway: Slightly more intake than exhaust is usually the easiest choice.
Positive pressure
more intake than exhaust
Negative pressure
more exhaust than intake
Beginner tip
use dust filters

CPU Cooling: Air vs Liquid

What you actually need
“Enough”

Air coolers are simple and reliable. Liquid coolers can look clean and cool well, but they add complexity. Most people are totally fine with a decent air cooler.

Simple takeaway: Air cooling is usually the best mix of cost, performance, and simplicity.
Air cooling
simple, reliable, quiet
Liquid cooling
cool + clean look, more parts
Beginner tip
start with air

Temps & Noise: What “Normal” Looks Like

Don’t chase perfect numbers
“Reality”

New builders often panic over temps. Realistically, modern parts are designed to run warm under load. What matters is: no constant overheating, no crashing, and no fans screaming 24/7.

Simple takeaway: “Stable and not obnoxiously loud” is the win.
Healthy goal
stable temps under load
Warning signs
shutdowns, throttling, crashes
Easy fix
recheck airflow path
