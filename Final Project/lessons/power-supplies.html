Power Supplies (PSU)

Wattage, efficiency, and how to avoid sketchy units.

The power supply is one of the least exciting parts of a PC.

What a PSU Actually Does

“Power + safety”
“Stability”

Your PSU converts wall power into stable power your PC can use. Your CPU and GPU don’t just want “enough watts,” They want clean, consistent power. That consistency is what separates a solid PSU from a sketchy one.

Simple takeaway: Don’t buy the cheapest PSU, it’s literally powering everything.
Main job
stable power conversion
Protects against
spikes, overloads (good units)
Bad PSU causes
crashes, shutdowns, instability

Wattage

How many watts do you need?
“Headroom”

Wattage is your PSU’s capacity. The mistake people make is either buying way too small (instability), or buying way too big “just in case” (wasted money).

A good rule is: choose a PSU that gives you comfortable headroom for your CPU + GPU, plus room for normal spikes. You don’t need perfection, just some breathing room.

Simple takeaway: Aim for “comfortably enough,” not “maximum possible.”
Too low
crashes / shutdowns under load
Too high
usually just costs more
Beginner tip
leave headroom for upgrades

Efficiency (80+ Ratings)

What Bronze/Gold actually means
“Heat”

The 80+ rating is mostly about efficiency: How much wall power gets wasted as heat. Higher efficiency usually means less heat and sometimes quieter operation, but it does not automatically mean “this PSU is high quality.”

Simple takeaway: 80+ is a clue, not a guarantee.
Bronze
fine for budget builds
Gold
common sweet spot
Platinum+
nice, usually not required

How to Avoid Sketchy Units

The red flags
“Safety”

Bad PSUs usually look fine in a product listing. The giveaway is usually the combination of: unknown brand, suspiciously low price, and vague specs.

Simple takeaway: If it seems too cheap for the wattage, assume there’s a reason.
Red flags
no reviews, weird branding, “Gaming PSU” only
Look for
clear model name + real specs
Better approach
buy known models, not wattage

Modular vs Semi vs Non-Modular

Cable convenience
“Clean”

Modular just means the cables can detach. It doesn’t change performance, it changes how clean and easy your cable management. Semi-modular is usually the best value.

Simple takeaway: Semi-modular is a great middle ground.
Non-modular
cheaper, more cable clutter
Semi-modular
value + cleaner routing
Fully modular
cleanest, usually costs more

Quick PSU Picking Checklist

A final sanity check before you buy
“Confirm”

If you want a simple “am I making a good decision?” moment, use this:

Quality model?
known brand + known model
Enough wattage?
comfortable headroom
Right connectors?
CPU 8-pin + GPU power
Simple takeaway: Buy the PSU you won’t have to think about again.
