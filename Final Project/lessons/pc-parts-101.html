PC Parts 101

Simply, what each part does.

This page breaks down core PC components into simple terms.

CPU

Central Processing Unit
“The brain”

The CPU handles instructions. In basic terms, it runs your operating system and coordinates what your programs do. Opening apps, loading websites, and clicking buttons all involve the CPU.

Simple takeaway: If your CPU is weak, your PC can feel “slow” even if everything else is decent.
What it affects
overall speed, multitasking
You’ll hear terms like
cores, threads, GHz
Beginner tip
match CPU to your use

RAM

Random Access Memory
“Short term memory”

RAM holds data your computer needs right now. More RAM helps with multitasking, like having lots of browser tabs open or multiple programs running at once.

Simple takeaway: RAM doesn’t “make everything faster” by itself, but not having enough makes everything feel worse.
What it affects
multitasking, smoothness
You’ll hear terms like
GB, speed (MHz), DDR4/DDR5
Beginner tip
16GB is a safe start

Storage

SSD / Hard Drive
“Long term”

Storage is where your files live permanently, your operating system, games, photos, and documents. Modern PCs use SSDs because they’re much faster than older hard drives.

Simple takeaway: An SSD is one of the biggest “it feels fast now” upgrades you can do.
What it affects
boot time, loading screens
You’ll hear terms like
NVMe, SATA, TB
Beginner tip
get SSD for OS

GPU

Graphics Processing Unit
“Graphics muscle”

The GPU renders graphics, games, 3D work, and anything that needs lots of visual processing. If you’re gaming or editing video, the GPU usually matters more than the CPU.

Simple takeaway: Your GPU mostly decides your gaming FPS and graphics quality.
What it affects
FPS, resolution, graphics settings
You’ll hear terms like
VRAM, drivers, ray tracing
Beginner tip
match GPU to your monitor

PSU

Power Supply Unit
“Power + safety”

The PSU converts wall power into stable power your PC can use. A good PSU protects your parts and keeps your system reliable, it’s not the place to cheap out.

Simple takeaway: A sketchy PSU can cause crashes or damage components.
What it affects
stability, upgrade headroom
You’ll hear terms like
watts, 80+ rating, modular
Beginner tip
buy quality, not just watts

Case / Chassis

The enclosure
“Airflow + fit”

The case holds everything together. It affects airflow, temperatures, noise, and whether your parts physically fit (GPU length, cooler height, motherboard size).

Simple takeaway: A good airflow case makes your build quieter and easier to cool.
What it affects
temps, noise, build ease
You’ll hear terms like
ATX/mATX/ITX, mesh front
Beginner tip
prioritize airflow + space

Motherboard

The main circuit board
“The connector”

The motherboard connects everything together: CPU, RAM, GPU, storage, and peripherals. It determines what parts are compatible and what features your system supports.

Simple takeaway: The motherboard doesn’t boost performance, but it decides what your PC can support.
What it affects
compatibility, expansion
You’ll hear terms like
chipset, socket, VRMs
Beginner tip
match CPU socket first

Cooling

CPU cooler & case fans
“Heat control”

Cooling keeps your components from overheating. This includes the CPU cooler and the fans that move air through your case. Good cooling improves performance stability and noise levels.

Simple takeaway: Better cooling means quieter operation and more consistent performance.
What it affects
temps, noise, longevity
You’ll hear terms like
air vs liquid, TDP
Beginner tip
air cooling is enough

Operating System

Windows / Linux
“The interface”

The operating system is the software that lets you interact with your PC. It manages hardware, runs programs, and provides the user interface you actually see and use.

Simple takeaway: Without an OS, your PC technically works, but you can’t do anything with it.
What it affects
software support, usability
You’ll hear terms like
drivers, activation
Beginner tip
Windows is simplest

Peripherals

Monitor / keyboard / mouse / audio
“Your experience”

Peripherals don’t go inside the PC, but they massively affect how the build feels. A great monitor can be more noticeable than a small GPU upgrade.

Simple takeaway: If you pair a strong PC with a weak monitor, it can easily bottleneck the experience. That goes for both gaming and productivity tasks, but it doesn't matter for general use.
What it affects
comfort, clarity, responsiveness
You’ll hear terms like
Hz, response time, DPI
Beginner tip
match monitor to your GPU
